## STRUCTURAL HIERARCHY

Infer the document's TRUE SEMANTIC HIERARCHY from numbering, numbering ancestry, siblings, surrounding structure, and document organization.

Do not rely on typography alone.

Determine parent-child relationships first, then assign Markdown heading levels according to RELATIVE structural depth.

Structural hierarchy has higher priority than font size, boldness, italics, indentation, centering, visual prominence, and physical page position.

Strong structural indicators include:

* major document titles such as QUYẾT ĐỊNH, QUY ĐỊNH, QUY CHẾ, HƯỚNG DẪN, THÔNG BÁO, KẾ HOẠCH, NỘI QUY;
* PHẦN;
* CHƯƠNG;
* Roman numeral sections such as I., II., III., IV.;
* Điều;
* hierarchical decimal identifiers such as 1.1, 1.2, 2.3;
* parenthesized hierarchical identifiers such as (1.1), (1.2), (5.1);
* deeper identifiers such as (1.3.1), (5.2.1), (5.2.6).

Heading levels are RELATIVE to the actual parent-child structure.

For example:

DOCUMENT TITLE
└── I.
└── 1.
└── (1.1)
└── (1.1.1)

should become:

# DOCUMENT TITLE

## I.

### 1.

#### (1.1)

##### (1.1.1)

However, do not permanently bind one numbering syntax to one Markdown heading level.

For example, `Điều 1.` may be directly under a document title in one document, but may be under CHƯƠNG in another document.

Assign heading levels from actual structural depth.

## NUMERIC ANCESTRY

Use numeric prefixes as one of the strongest signals for parent-child hierarchy.

Examples:

(1.3.1) is a direct child of (1.3).

(5.2.1) is a direct child of (5.2).

(5.2.6) is a sibling of (5.2.1).

(5.1), (5.2), and (5.3) are siblings under section 5.

(1.1), (1.2), and (1.3) are siblings under section 1 when the surrounding structure supports that interpretation.

For a hierarchy such as:

5.

├── (5.1)
│   ├── (5.1.1)
│   └── (5.1.2)
├── (5.2)
│   ├── (5.2.1)
│   └── (5.2.2)
└── (5.3)
├── (5.3.1)
└── (5.3.2)

all `(5.x)` nodes are siblings and MUST use the same Markdown heading level.

All `(5.x.y)` nodes are children of their matching `(5.x)` parent.

## STRICT PARENT-CHILD DEPTH

A direct structural child MUST be exactly one Markdown heading level deeper than its direct structural parent.

For example:

If:

#### (5.1)

then its direct children must be:

##### (5.1.1)

##### (5.1.2)

##### (5.1.3)

If:

#### (5.2)

then its direct children must be:

##### (5.2.1)

##### (5.2.2)

##### (5.2.3)

If:

#### (5.3)

then its direct children must be:

##### (5.3.1)

##### (5.3.2)

##### (5.3.3)

Never output a direct parent and its numeric child at the same heading level.

Incorrect:

#### (5.2)

#### (5.2.1)

Correct:

#### (5.2)

##### (5.2.1)

This rule applies consistently to any hierarchical numbering pattern.

## SIBLING CONSISTENCY

Structural siblings sharing the same parent and numbering family MUST use the same Markdown heading level.

For example:

(5.1)
(5.2)
(5.3)

must use the same structural level when they are siblings under section 5.

Likewise:

(5.2.1)
(5.2.2)
(5.2.3)

must use the same structural level.

Never produce inconsistent sibling structures such as:

#### (5.1)

### (5.2)

#### (5.3)

when all three are siblings.

If one item in a sequential sibling family has already established the structural level, preserve that level for the remaining matching siblings unless there is strong evidence of a different parent.

## CROSS-PAGE SIBLING CONTINUITY

A physical page boundary MUST NOT reset, promote, demote, or otherwise change an established hierarchy.

If a numbered sibling sequence continues across a page boundary, the items after the page break MUST retain exactly the same Markdown heading level as the matching siblings before the page break.

Example:

##### (5.3.1)

[PAGE BREAK]

(5.3.2)
(5.3.3)
(5.3.4)

MUST become:

##### (5.3.1)

##### (5.3.2)

##### (5.3.3)

##### (5.3.4)

It is incorrect to output:

##### (5.3.1)

#### (5.3.2)

or:

##### (5.3.1)

(5.3.2)

A page break does not create a new parent and does not reset numbering ancestry.

Before assigning a heading level to a numbered item after a page break, inspect the active hierarchy and its matching numbered siblings from the preceding page.

## OPERATIVE ARTICLES

A sequential group of Điều 1., Điều 2., Điều 3., ... containing operative provisions is structural.

When multiple Điều siblings occur sequentially, represent them as Markdown headings at the same structural level.

Their heading level must be determined RELATIVELY from their actual parent.

For example, when Điều 1., Điều 2., Điều 3. are direct children of a QUYẾT ĐỊNH document, they should be sibling headings directly below that document.

Administrative authority names, recipient blocks, dates, signatures, document numbers, or similar metadata do NOT automatically create a structural parent for an Điều sequence.

Do not let visual formatting of administrative authority lines change the structural parent of Điều items.

## AMBIGUOUS SIMPLE NUMBERING

Patterns such as:

1.
2.
3.

A.
B.
a.

may represent either structural headings or ordinary list items.

Do NOT classify them solely from syntax.

Treat them as structural headings only when surrounding evidence supports that interpretation.

Useful structural evidence includes:

* sequential sibling numbering;
* subordinate numbered content;
* an established parent section;
* short section-like wording;
* substantial content introduced below the item;
* consistent formatting with sibling structural sections.

A simple numbered item that contains subordinate identifiers is strong evidence that it is structural.

For example:

1. Tiêu chuẩn Đạo đức tốt

(1.1) ...
(1.2) ...
(1.3) ...

should be interpreted structurally as:

### 1. Tiêu chuẩn Đạo đức tốt

#### (1.1) ...

#### (1.2) ...

#### (1.3) ...

Patterns such as:

1.

A)
A/
a)
a/

are ordinary list items by default.

`a.` is also an ordinary item by default unless surrounding document structure provides unusually strong evidence that it is a structural section.

Do not convert ordinary list items into headings merely because they are short, bold, indented, or visually emphasized.

## PAGE FURNITURE

Physical page numbers, headers, footers, and similar page furniture are not semantic structural nodes.

Do not use them to reset hierarchy.

Do not promote or demote surrounding headings because of page furniture.

If an isolated printed page number is clearly only page furniture, do not treat it as document content or as a structural section.

## TABLES

Preserve actual tables as Markdown tables.

Keep tables attached to the structural section or criterion that introduces them.

Do not let a table interrupt or reset the active hierarchy.

If a section continues after a table, preserve the same parent-child structure.

## FLOWCHARTS AND WORKFLOW TABLES

Detect sections titled LƯU ĐỒ, QUY TRÌNH, WORKFLOW, PROCESS, or similar.

A bordered structure may be both:
- a table containing step metadata; and
- a flowchart containing nodes, arrows, branches, merges, or loops.

Treat it as a HYBRID WORKFLOW TABLE.

### GRAPH RECONSTRUCTION

Read the workflow from visible shapes, connector lines, and arrowheads.

Do not determine the workflow from row order alone.

Preserve every visible directed connection, including:
- vertical transitions;
- side branches;
- branches that skip rows;
- paths that merge into another step;
- loops returning to an earlier step.

If one node has multiple outgoing arrows, list every destination.

If multiple arrows enter one node, preserve every incoming path.

Never invent:
- branch conditions;
- Yes/No labels;
- missing steps;
- arrow directions;
- relationships not visibly supported by the document.

When a branch has no printed condition, preserve the connection without adding a condition.

### NODE TYPES

Infer node type only from its visible shape:

- ellipse or rounded terminator: start/end;
- rectangle: process;
- diamond: decision;
- other or unclear shape: unspecified.

A diamond does not automatically imply Yes/No branches unless those labels are printed.

### TABLE ROW MAPPING

Map every flowchart node to its corresponding TT or step row.

Keep all metadata attached to that step:

- Nội dung công việc;
- Người thực hiện;
- Thời gian thực hiện;
- Ghi chú;
- other visible columns.

Do not separate a node from metadata in the same row.

### MARKDOWN OUTPUT

Represent the workflow once, using:

1. A compact flow line containing every visible edge.
2. A Markdown table containing step details and outgoing destinations.

Required format:

### <workflow title>

**Luồng:** `1 -> 2; 2 -> 3; 3 -> 4; 3 -> 5; 4 -> 5`

| Bước | Loại nút | Công việc | Nội dung công việc | Người thực hiện | Thời gian | Ghi chú | Bước tiếp theo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use semicolons when a node has multiple destinations.

If a connector cannot be determined reliably, write `không xác định`.
Do not silently infer an edge from numbering.

### CONSISTENCY CHECK

Before returning Markdown, verify:

1. Every visible node appears exactly once.
2. Every visible arrow appears in the flow line and `Bước tiếp theo`.
3. Branches and merges are preserved.
4. No unprinted branch condition is invented.
5. Every step retains metadata from its original table row.

## FINAL CONSISTENCY CHECK

Before returning Markdown, verify the document hierarchy globally:

1. Every structural child is deeper than its parent.
2. A direct structural child is exactly one Markdown heading level deeper than its direct parent.
3. Structural siblings use the same Markdown heading level.
4. Numeric prefixes preserve parent-child ancestry.
5. Page boundaries do not reset hierarchy.
6. Continued numbered siblings after a page break retain the same heading level as siblings before the break.
7. A numbering sequence is interpreted consistently throughout the document.
8. Điều siblings are represented consistently when they form operative structural sections.
9. Ambiguous simple numbering is promoted only when contextual evidence supports section status.
10. Tables and page furniture do not alter the semantic hierarchy.

Return the parsed document as Markdown.
